I have read every edition of the Technology Radar since about 2017, and I have watched a lot
of companies try to build their own. Almost all of the internal ones die within two editions.
It took me a while to work out why, because the format looks trivially easy to copy.

Here is what I think is actually load-bearing.

## It puts things in Hold

Most internal radars are a list of things the platform team would like you to use. That is not
a radar, it is a roadmap with better typography.

The Hold ring is the whole trick. Naming a popular practice and saying *stop* is the only part
of the exercise that costs anything, and cost is what makes a position credible. A document
that only recommends is free to produce and worth roughly what it cost.

## It is dated

Every blip belongs to an edition. That sounds administrative and it is the second most
important property, because it converts a claim into a track record. You can go back and see
that they put a thing in Assess in 2019 and moved it to Hold in 2022, and judge them on that.

Undated opinions cannot be wrong. Which is another way of saying they cannot be right either.

## It is short

A blip is a paragraph. The discipline of a paragraph forces the author to have one reason
rather than five caveats. I notice that the blips I remember are the ones where they clearly
had to cut something.

## Where I think it is weak

The quadrants have never been very good. Techniques, Tools, Platforms, Languages and
Frameworks is a taxonomy of implementation, and a lot of the most interesting blips are
really about how people work. They keep having to file organisational practices under
"Techniques", which is a bin rather than a category.

The other weakness is inherited by everyone who copies it: a radar describes positions, not
sequence. Knowing that four things are in Adopt tells you nothing about which to do first,
and for most teams ordering is the actual problem.

## What I took from it

I built [a radar of my own](/radar) for product practice. Four rings, the same rules, and the
two constraints I think matter most: something has to be in Hold, and every position carries a
date.

If you are thinking of doing the same, my advice is to write the Hold items first. If you
cannot fill that ring, you do not have a point of view yet, you have a preferences list.
